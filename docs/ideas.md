Good choice. Angry Birds has a strong core: aim, launch, watch it collapse. But the strategy is shallow:

```
 Angry Birds:  fling ──► random collapse ──► retry until lucky
```
- Results often come down to luck, not planning.
- Enemies just sit there.
- You rarely plan more than one shot ahead.

Here's a version with the strategy and the grown-up tone turned way up.

## "Siege" 🏰💣

### 1. Plan before you fire
You get a **budget**, not a set list of shots.
```
 $1000 to spend:
  [Heavy shell $300]  [Drill round $200]  [Incendiary $150]  [Scout drone $100]
```
Do you buy a scout drone first to find weak points, or spend it all on firepower?

### 2. Buildings stand up for real reasons
- Every structure has **load-bearing beams**. Hit the right one and the whole thing falls; hit the wrong one and you just chip a wall.
- Materials behave differently: wood burns, concrete cracks, steel bends.
- **Skill beats luck:** good players read the structure like an engineer.

### 3. The enemy fights back between your shots
```
 you fire ──► enemy reacts ──► you fire again
               ├─ repairs walls
               ├─ moves troops to cover
               └─ fires back at your cannon
```
It plays like chess with explosions. Waiting costs you, because they rebuild.

### 4. Your choices have consequences
- Civilians or neighboring buildings nearby: **collateral damage lowers your score**. That's where the grown-up tone comes from.
- Some missions require taking out just one target, cleanly.
- Wind, distance, and bullet drop all affect your shots, the same sniping skill as the speed game.

### 5. Both sides can build (later)
You design a fortress, other players try to bring it down, and you study how they did it to build better.

## How it connects to your other ideas
```
 Same physics + shooting engine
   ├─ Speedrun sniper  (fast, reflexes)
   └─ Siege            (slow, planning)
```
Both use the same core skill: **reading physics and placing one perfect shot.** You could even make them two modes of one game.

**To build first:** a single 2D Siege level with one fortress, 3 ammo types, a budget, and load-bearing beams. That's enough to test whether the planning feels good.
